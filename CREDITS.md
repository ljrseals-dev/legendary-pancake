# Credits

Marked "read" were looked at online while building; the rest were written from memory and not re-checked.

- Kelly, J. L., and Lochbaum, C. C. (1962). Speech synthesis. Proceedings of the Fourth International Congress on Acoustics, Copenhagen, G42. The tube model of the vocal tract. From memory.
- Hass, J. Synthesis, chapter 4, speech synthesis and the channel vocoder, page 6. Indiana University, Center for Electronic and Computer Music. https://cmtext.com/synthesis/chapter4_speech_vocoder6.php. **Read**: Kelly, Lochbaum and Max Mathews, the IBM 704, "Daisy", 1961 and the 1962 publication.
- Guinness World Records, first song performed using computer speech synthesis. **Read**: credits Carol Lochbaum and John Kelly at Bell Labs, Murray Hill, IBM 704, and dates it 1960 (the Hass page says 1961).
- Rosenberg, A. E. (1971). Effect of glottal pulse shape on the quality of natural vowels. JASA 49(2B). From memory.
- Sundberg, J. (1974). Articulatory interpretation of the "singing formant". JASA 55(4). From memory; used only for the idea of a narrow epilarynx tube over a wide pharynx.
- Peterson, G. E., and Barney, H. L. (1952). Control methods used in a study of the vowels. JASA 24(2). From memory; the fitting targets are in the style of this study, not copied from it.
- Daisy Bell (Harry Dacre, 1892), public domain. The pitches and rhythm follow a public BASIC transcription: https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~arpepper/Cycling/daisy.html (**read**). The syllable alignment and vowel assignment are the author's.

Code is original to this project; `src/dsp.js` is shared with Sweep Capture.
